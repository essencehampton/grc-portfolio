# How I Think About Risk
*Brightpath Health is a fictional company created for this portfolio.*

## My one-paragraph definition of GRC
GRC consists of governance, and how a company decides or directs security, who owns what, and also how leaders approve risk. It also consists of risk, and how searching for what goes wrong and how likely or bad that risk is. Lastly, GRC consists of compliance, and proving that a standard is met.

## The four risk responses, with Brightpath examples
- Mitigate: Brightpath hasn't enforced MFA in Microsoft 365, so it turns on MFA for all admin accounts to reduce the chance of stolen passwords being used.
- Transfer: Brightpath buys cyber insurance to cover breach costs like patient notification and legal fees.
- Avoid: Marketing wanted to use an unapproved file-sharing app for customer lists. It hadn't been through a security review, so Brightpath denied it and requires the approved company tool instead.
- Accept: Some older laptops can't support MFA until they're replaced. The CISO accepted this risk until March 31, 2027, as long as the devices stay on a separate network and their logins are monitored.

## One control of each kind
| Control | Function (prevent/detect/correct) | Type (admin/technical/physical) |
|---|---|---|
| a fire drill | Preventive | Administrative |
| a smoke detector | Detective | Physical |
| Restoring data from backups after ransomware | Corrective | Technical |

## Where GRC sits
The first line of the three line model is with the business and IT teams that own and run controls, the second line is GRC, risk, and compliance, that sets the standard and monitors them, and the third line is internal audit, who checks that the other two lines work. GRC sits in between making sure the right controls actually exists and work, but we do not run the firewall.
